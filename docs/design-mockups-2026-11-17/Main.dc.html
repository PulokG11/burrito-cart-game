Burrito Cart

A review of the live prototype next to the inspiration game, and a direction that gives Salsa Springs a look of its own. These are mockups only; nothing in the game has changed.

Mockups use the real Day 1 and Heat wave data, and scores computed by the game's own engine.

The game today

Keep

  • Profit updates live as trucks move.
  • Tips pinned to the truck they talk about.
  • Every spot reachable by keyboard, with spoken labels.
  • Each day re-scored and saved by the server.
  • The solver runs on the player's own device and says how fast it was.

From the inspiration

Borrow: a city you can navigate by its landmarks, bold red demand badges, the profit equation in the toolbar, a round and day panel, a minimap, and range bars in Round 2.

Leave: the mint isometric art and branding, which are Gurobi's, and the "−7 | +7" offset labels, which make players do sums. Label the actual low and high instead.

Fix

Now Proposed
Looks like any web app: cream page, terracotta accent, stacked rounded cards. A town with its own palette: talavera cobalt, chile, marigold and painted façades. One cobalt panel replaces the cards.
Buildings are identical tiles; the pun names hide in tooltips. Each building gets a rooftop sign (a loaf for Bayes Bakery, waves for Max-Flow Waterpark). A Names switch labels every pun.
Demand numbers around 9 px; truck spots are faint 4 px dots. 32 px chile badges that keep their size at any zoom. Spots become yellow parking bays painted on the street.
Walking distance, the core rule, is described in text. A dragged truck shows how far customers will walk to it. The building panel plots the walking curve with this building on it.
Round 2 ranges are grey text. Range bars drawn to scale, marking the forecast and the average; after Done, the actual lands on the bar.
Results split into two half-size maps. One full map: your trucks solid, the best plan as outlines, a switch for either or both, and tips pinned with arrows.
Profit shown as four separate numbers. Written as the equation the game teaches: profit = sales − ingredients − trucks.
On a phone the whole map shrinks to 340 px. A zoomed map you pan, a minimap showing where you are, and the equation in a bottom sheet.

Principles

The city is the interface. Players should say "put one by the stadium", not "spot S14".

Show the rule, don't explain it. Walking reach, forecast spread and the solver's choice are drawn on the map.

The map is the loud part. Panels stay quiet: one cobalt surface, hairline dividers, no card stacks.
